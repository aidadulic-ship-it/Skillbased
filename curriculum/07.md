# 07 · Skills-Based Learning Design

Editorial draft — source verification pending

**Learning outcome** — Design practice that transfers to real work and collect evidence of its effect.

**Skill** — Skills-Based Learning Design. Design measurable practice and evaluate transfer.

**Scope boundary** — Accredited qualification design.

**Practice evidence** — Practice and evaluation plan.

## Lessons

### Needs

Compare expected behavior with observed behavior and diagnose whether instruction can close the gap. Examine tools, incentives, time and supervision.

**Example** — If workers know the procedure but lack access to logs, resolve access before training.

**Limitation** — Interviews alone may understate workflow barriers.

**Knowledge check** — When is training unlikely to help?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

When people can perform the work but the environment prevents its use.

</details>

### Objectives

Describe the behavior, conditions and evidence standard. Align practice and feedback to that behavior so learners can judge success.

**Example** — Given a synthetic alarm history, identify the likely subsystem and record a safe escalation step.

**Limitation** — A convenient quiz may test recall instead of the required judgment.

**Knowledge check** — What makes an objective observable?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

A defined action that produces evidence a reviewer can inspect.

</details>

### Deliberate practice

Provide repeated tasks at increasing complexity, specific feedback and opportunities to correct errors. Vary examples to prevent memorizing one solution.

**Example** — Practice with three alarm patterns and explain signs that would invalidate a diagnosis.

**Limitation** — Repetition without feedback can reinforce errors.

**Knowledge check** — What is a productive response to a repeated error?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Give feedback linked to the decision point and offer a targeted retry.

</details>

### Transfer

Plan when and where learners will apply the skill with coaching and tools. Ask managers to remove barriers and observe representative work.

**Example** — Reserve a supervised maintenance shift and provide a checklist at the point of work.

**Limitation** — Access to real work may be uneven across participants.

**Knowledge check** — What is a transfer opportunity?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

A supported chance to apply the learned behavior in relevant work conditions.

</details>

### Evaluation

Track participation, learning evidence and changes in work separately. Consider baselines, comparison conditions and alternative explanations.

**Example** — Compare diagnostic decisions on unfamiliar examples and observe supervised use after the course.

**Limitation** — Before-and-after improvements may reflect equipment changes or easier cases.

**Knowledge check** — What does attendance establish?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Participation, with no direct evidence of learning or transfer.

</details>

## Challenges

### Write measurable outcomes

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

A module goal says understand alarms.

**Work product** — Two behavioral objectives with conditions and observable evidence.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Describe the decision a learner must make.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Specify behavior, conditions and evidence. Reconsider your approach. Describe the decision a learner must make.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>

### Compare interventions
