# 04 · Skills Gap Analysis

Editorial draft — source verification pending

**Learning outcome** — Prioritize missing capability while exposing uncertainty in supply and demand.

**Skill** — Skills Gap Analysis. Estimate coverage gaps with explicit evidence and uncertainty.

**Scope boundary** — Verified competence of individuals.

**Practice evidence** — Prioritized gap analysis.

## Lessons

### Future demand

Estimate critical workload under explicit scenarios and translate it into coverage requirements. State assumptions about timing, work design and productivity.

**Example** — Model two shifts of troubleshooting coverage after NOVA adds automated cells.

**Limitation** — A forecast tied to unapproved equipment investment may overstate demand.

**Knowledge check** — What should accompany a demand estimate?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

A time horizon and the operating assumptions behind the estimate.

</details>

### Evidence quality

Assess recency, relevance, corroboration and missingness before classifying available skill. Record how evidence was collected and who had access to opportunities.

**Example** — A recent observed fault diagnosis provides more direct evidence than an annual self-rating for that task.

**Limitation** — Observation can still omit rare faults and introduce reviewer effects.

**Knowledge check** — How should a missing observation be recorded?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

As unknown, with a plan to collect evidence if the distinction matters.

</details>

### Current supply

Count available coverage in the context of shifts, permissions and workload. Keep potential capacity distinct from confirmed coverage.

**Example** — NOVA has two trained technicians on a roster, but both work days and one mentors full time.

**Limitation** — Today’s availability can change with leave or rotations.

**Knowledge check** — Can two trained people guarantee two-shift coverage?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

No. Availability, authorizations and schedule alignment must be verified.

</details>

### Gaps and uncertainty

Report a plausible range, assumptions and the effect of uncertain records. Decide whether more measurement could change the intervention.

**Example** — The night shift may lack one to two qualified participants, depending on validation of outdated evidence.

**Limitation** — A range can still be misleading if it ignores an alternative work design.

**Knowledge check** — What distinguishes no evidence from evidence of absence?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Missing evidence does not establish whether someone can perform the skill.

</details>

### Intervention priority

Compare operational impact, urgency, confidence and feasibility. Prioritize actions that improve coverage or reduce consequential uncertainty within capacity.

**Example** — Run a supervised diagnostic exercise before committing to external hiring.

**Limitation** — A low-cost measurement activity cannot replace immediate coverage for critical duties.

**Knowledge check** — When should a gap trigger action before full validation?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

When a credible near-term risk requires a reversible safeguard or immediate coverage.

</details>

## Challenges

### Audit the inputs

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

NOVA’s skill spreadsheet includes expired records and blank cells.

**Work product** — A data-quality register with decision impact and collection actions.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Check who was eligible to produce evidence.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Separate missing and stale evidence from demonstrated gaps. Reconsider your approach. Check who was eligible to produce evidence.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>

### Prioritize coverage gaps

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

A new cell launches in eight weeks and the shifts have uneven evidence.

**Work product** — A prioritized gap table with uncertainty and interim coverage.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rank critical work before convenient measurement.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

</details>

### Challenge false certainty

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

Leadership quotes a single gap percentage from mixed self-ratings and observations.

**Work product** — A revised statement with assumptions, a range and a decision threshold.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Ask which assumption would change the decision.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Show uncertainty and validate high-impact records. Reconsider your approach. Ask which assumption would change the decision.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>
