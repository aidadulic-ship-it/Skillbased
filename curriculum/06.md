# 06 · Internal Talent Mobility

Editorial draft — source verification pending

**Learning outcome** — Design transparent access to work opportunities and a fair matching process.

**Skill** — Internal Talent Mobility. Build transparent matching, selection and release processes.

**Scope boundary** — Individual appointment authority.

**Practice evidence** — Opportunity and selection rubric.

## Lessons

### Matching

Match opportunity requirements with relevant evidence, availability and learner interest. Separate essential constraints from capabilities an assignment can develop.

**Example** — A diagnostic shadowing assignment requires safe working induction and can develop alarm interpretation.

**Limitation** — Strict matching can exclude people who could learn with reasonable supervision.

**Knowledge check** — What should be essential for a shadowing opportunity?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Necessary safety conditions and availability, plus any genuinely required prior knowledge.

</details>

### Opportunity transparency

Publish the learning value, availability, selection criteria and timeline in channels workers can access. Make application and release steps clear.

**Example** — NOVA posts opportunities where both shifts can see them and allows a defined application window.

**Limitation** — Publication alone may not reach workers with limited digital access.

**Knowledge check** — How could you check that both shifts saw the opportunity?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Review communication reach by shift and ask workers how they received the posting.

</details>

### Evidence and fairness

Use comparable prompts and relevant evidence, offer alternative evidence routes and audit who is eligible, shortlisted and selected.

**Example** — Accept a supervised work sample from workers without prior project access.

**Limitation** — Small samples can obscure disparities and expose individuals if published.

**Knowledge check** — Why record the number of eligible applicants?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Selection counts alone cannot show where access or drop-off differs.

</details>

### Manager incentives

Identify what managers lose when people move and plan backfill, handovers and incentives. Establish a decision deadline for release disputes.

**Example** — A site temporarily shares mentor time and schedules a backfill before releasing an operator.

**Limitation** — Overly rigid release dates can create genuine safety or service risks.

**Knowledge check** — What distinguishes a capacity constraint from indefinite blocking?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

A documented operational reason with a time-bounded mitigation and review.

</details>

### Process design

Map the steps from posting to selection, release, support and review. Assign an owner at each handoff and keep a correction and escalation channel.

**Example** — HR checks application access while line managers confirm the handover schedule.

**Limitation** — A formal process can be heavy for short assignments.

**Knowledge check** — Which step should follow selection?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Confirm assignment terms, support and release timing with the employee and managers.

</details>

## Challenges

### Diagnose hidden barriers

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

Applicants come almost entirely from day shift even though night staff are eligible.

**Work product** — A barrier map and a measurement plan across application stages.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare exposure, scheduling and selection separately.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Check reach and practical access by shift. Reconsider your approach. Compare exposure, scheduling and selection separately.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>

### Design fair matching

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

NOVA offers one supervised project with multiple eligible applicants.

**Work product** — A posting, evidence rubric and tie-handling procedure.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Allow equivalent evidence when prior opportunities differ.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.

</details>

### Resolve release conflict

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

An employee is selected but their manager cites a production peak.

**Work product** — A release decision, contingency and escalation date.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Verify the date and coverage constraint.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Agree a bounded handover with a review deadline. Reconsider your approach. Verify the date and coverage constraint.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>
