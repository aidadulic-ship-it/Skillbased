# 00 · SBO Foundations

Editorial draft — source verification pending

**Learning outcome** — Decide where skills information can improve a concrete work decision.

## Lessons

### Definition and limits

A skills-informed organization uses evidence about what people can do to inform how work is staffed, developed or redesigned. Start by identifying who makes the decision, what evidence they already have and what gap a skills signal would fill.

**Example** — NOVA can allocate a supervised troubleshooting exercise using demonstrated diagnostic skills instead of job title alone.

**Limitation** — A skills profile cannot substitute for statutory qualifications or remove a manager’s accountability.

**Knowledge check** — What must be defined before collecting skill profiles?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

The decision the profiles will support and the evidence the decision requires.

</details>

### Jobs, roles, tasks and skills

A job groups formal employment responsibilities. A role describes a contribution in a setting. A task is a unit of work. A skill is a learned capacity that can transfer across tasks under comparable conditions.

**Example** — Maintenance technician is a job, shift incident lead is a role, isolating a fault is a task and interpreting alarm histories is a skill.

**Limitation** — A single observed task may reflect tools, team support or luck as well as individual skill.

**Knowledge check** — Classify interpreting alarm histories and leading a shift incident.

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Interpreting alarm histories is a skill. Leading a shift incident describes a role if it denotes ongoing responsibility.

</details>

### Strategic reasons

Connect the desired operating result to a critical activity, then ask whether a change in skill availability could plausibly improve it. Specify the baseline and the mechanism before committing to any taxonomy.

**Example** — NOVA compares repair delays by shift and finds that few people on nights can diagnose recurring sensor faults.

**Limitation** — If parts arrive late, training more diagnosticians may leave downtime unchanged.

**Knowledge check** — Name one competing explanation for long repair times.

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Parts availability, equipment condition, staffing coverage or unclear escalation could explain long repair times.

</details>

### Risks and counterarguments

Skills evidence can become stale, unevenly collected or misused for employment decisions. Evaluate the impact from business, HR and employee perspectives and provide a correction route before expanding collection.

**Example** — NOVA restricts practice records to pilot coaching and lets employees correct a mislabeled work sample.

**Limitation** — Limiting access can reduce the value of sharing. Measure whether the permitted use produces enough value on its own.

**Knowledge check** — How could a profile disadvantage a capable employee?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

A worker with fewer opportunities to demonstrate a skill may appear less capable because the profile lacks evidence.

</details>

### Job-oriented, hybrid or skills-oriented

Choose the operating model per decision. Job-based accountability suits stable or regulated work. A hybrid approach keeps formal jobs while adding skills signals to selected decisions. Broader skills-based staffing requires stronger evidence and coordination.

**Example** — NOVA keeps authorized maintenance roles while using a narrow skill inventory to choose supervised development assignments.

**Limitation** — Even a small pilot has maintenance costs. Retain the current process if it already meets the decision need.

**Knowledge check** — What should stay in the formal job description in a hybrid model?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Accountable duties, authority and mandatory qualifications should remain explicit.

</details>

## Challenges

### Classify work at NOVA

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

A maintenance document lists technician, incident lead, investigate a pressure drop and read a pressure trend.

**Work product** — A four-row classification with reasons and one ambiguous example.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Distinguish ongoing accountability from a single work activity.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Treat a job and a role as separate concepts. Reconsider your approach. Distinguish ongoing accountability from a single work activity.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>

### Diagnose a business problem

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

Night-shift repairs take longer. Leadership wants to start with an enterprise taxonomy.

**Work product** — A problem statement, two explanations, evidence to test them and a bounded next step.

### Advise on job descriptions

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

The COO proposes replacing regulated maintenance job descriptions with skills profiles.

**Work product** — A recommendation that describes duties, evidence uses and worker safeguards.
