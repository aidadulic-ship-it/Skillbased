# 01 · Skills-Based Strategy

Editorial draft — source verification pending

**Learning outcome** — Turn a business problem into a bounded, testable skills strategy.

**Skill** — Skills-Based Strategy. Connect an operating problem to a testable skills intervention.

**Scope boundary** — Enterprise strategy approval.

**Practice evidence** — Pilot business case.

## Lessons

### Business problem first

Define the undesirable outcome, who experiences it and when it occurs. Separate the symptom from possible causes and collect enough data to reject the most plausible alternatives.

**Example** — Compare NOVA downtime by asset and shift before attributing it to a shortage.

**Limitation** — Averages may conceal a single failing asset.

**Knowledge check** — What would weaken the skills-shortage hypothesis?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Comparable delays where qualified staff are present and required parts are unavailable.

</details>

### Strategy-to-skills alignment

Translate an operating objective into recurring critical work and observable evidence of capability. Select a skill only when its availability can plausibly change execution.

**Example** — Link faster line recovery to interpreting alarms and choosing a safe first diagnostic step.

**Limitation** — A skill can be relevant without being the binding constraint.

**Knowledge check** — What evidence connects a skill to line recovery?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Observed diagnostic work plus baseline and follow-up recovery times with relevant operating conditions.

</details>

### Transformation options

Compare the current process, a narrow hybrid pilot and wider redesign using the same outcome, implementation costs and risks. Prefer a reversible change when uncertainty is high.

**Example** — Test skills-informed shadow assignments on one shift before changing internal job posting rules.

**Limitation** — A narrow pilot may miss coordination costs at greater scale.

**Knowledge check** — What makes a pilot reversible?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

A defined end date, retained existing process and a documented decision for removing temporary data and rules.

</details>

### Costs and uncertainty

Separate direct costs from opportunity costs and estimate a range of plausible benefits. Specify a break-even condition and where assumptions need verification.

**Example** — NOVA budgets mentor time and evaluates savings across low and high downtime estimates.

**Limitation** — Savings may fail to materialize if demand or production schedules change.

**Knowledge check** — Name an opportunity cost of coaching.

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

The work a mentor cannot complete while supervising practice.

</details>

### Stakeholder tradeoffs

Document what each stakeholder gains, risks and can influence. Design the smallest agreement that preserves performance, manageable workloads and a fair employee experience.

**Example** — Workers receive paid practice time, supervisors define capacity limits and operations owns downtime measurement.

**Limitation** — Agreement at launch does not guarantee support when workloads shift.

**Knowledge check** — What would make a mobility pilot costly for a line manager?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Losing trained staff without backfill or a realistic handover plan.

</details>

### One-page blueprint

Record the problem, hypothesis, intervention, owner, metric, safeguards and stop criteria together. Use the blueprint to expose contradictory assumptions during approval.

**Example** — The pilot runs six months, uses supervised practice and pauses after any safety process breach.

**Limitation** — A blueprint is only useful if owners can update assumptions as new observations arrive.

**Knowledge check** — Why predefine a stop rule?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

It gives reviewers an agreed basis for stopping before sunk costs or pressure distort the decision.

</details>

## Challenges

### Diagnose the shortage

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

NOVA has open positions and repair delays, but the records mix vacancies, scheduling and diagnostic skill.

**Work product** — A causal map and three data requests ranked by decision value.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Separate headcount availability from demonstrated skill.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Compare scheduling, equipment and skill hypotheses. Reconsider your approach. Separate headcount availability from demonstrated skill.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>

### Pilot business case

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

Finance asks whether to support a six-month maintenance pilot with limited mentoring capacity.

**Work product** — A one-page case with a baseline, cost range, risk and stop rule.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count time away from productive work.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Use a range and specify a break-even condition. Reconsider your approach. Count time away from productive work.

</details>

### Defend the strategy

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

The CEO wants speed, managers fear losing capacity and workers worry about surveillance.

**Work product** — A decision memo with one actionable response per stakeholder.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Link safeguards to who owns the decision.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Address concerns with workload and data controls. Reconsider your approach. Link safeguards to who owns the decision.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>
