# 05 · Strategic Workforce Planning

Editorial draft — source verification pending

**Learning outcome** — Compare ways to cover future work under competing scenarios.

**Skill** — Strategic Workforce Planning. Translate work scenarios into options for capacity and coverage.

**Scope boundary** — Binding hiring decisions.

**Practice evidence** — Scenario and trigger plan.

## Lessons

### Critical work

Identify work that limits service, safety or strategic outcomes. Examine the actual activity and timing before translating it into headcount or skills demand.

**Example** — NOVA identifies safe after-hours diagnostics as critical to shift recovery.

**Limitation** — Work criticality varies by operating context and failure mode.

**Knowledge check** — What precedes a headcount estimate?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

A description of the work, timing and coverage requirement.

</details>

### Supply and demand

Compare feasible capacity with workload using the same time and unit. Allow for leave, induction and time unavailable for productive work.

**Example** — Calculate diagnostic coverage in supported shifts instead of comparing annual employee totals.

**Limitation** — A demand estimate can miss sudden peaks during equipment transitions.

**Knowledge check** — Why use the same unit?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

It prevents apparent gaps created by comparing incompatible measures.

</details>

### Scenarios

Construct internally consistent alternatives with different drivers. Name indicators that distinguish scenarios early enough to respond.

**Example** — NOVA compares a delayed installation with accelerated rollout triggered by the vendor schedule.

**Limitation** — Scenarios explore plausible futures and do not assign probabilities without evidence.

**Knowledge check** — What makes an indicator actionable?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

It arrives before the decision deadline and has a defined decision response.

</details>

### Intervention tradeoffs

Compare building capability, buying it, borrowing capacity and automating work. Evaluate speed, total cost, reliability and how easily the decision can be reversed.

**Example** — Contract short-term support and pair it with internal coaching during installation.

**Limitation** — Contractors may introduce dependency or have limited authorization.

**Knowledge check** — What should an automation option include in its cost?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Integration, oversight, maintenance, exception handling and fallback.

</details>

### Monitoring

Specify what is measured, who checks it and when action is required. Include leading indicators and review whether assumptions still hold.

**Example** — Review mentoring availability weekly until both shifts reach supervised coverage.

**Limitation** — Measurements that arrive monthly cannot guide a decision due tomorrow.

**Knowledge check** — Name one leading indicator for internal development.

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Scheduled and completed supervised practice slots.

</details>

## Challenges

### Derive planning questions

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

NOVA leadership wants a hiring number before approving a rollout schedule.

**Work product** — Five planning questions tied to milestones and affected work.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Ask what work changes under each rollout date.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Define workload and coverage before headcount. Reconsider your approach. Ask what work changes under each rollout date.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>
