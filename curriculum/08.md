# 08 · Evidence-Based Skills Assessment

Editorial draft — source verification pending

**Learning outcome** — Judge evidence with anchored criteria and preserve a route to correction.

**Skill** — Evidence-Based Skills Assessment. Assess learning evidence with anchored criteria and review.

**Scope boundary** — Formal occupational certification.

**Practice evidence** — Calibrated rubric and appeal record.

## Lessons

### Evidence types

Assess evidence by how directly it supports the intended inference. Combine self-report for discovery, structured tests for specific knowledge and work samples for performance under stated conditions.

**Example** — Compare self-ratings with an observed mock incident and a documented prior work sample.

**Limitation** — A work sample reflects particular conditions and may omit help received.

**Knowledge check** — Which evidence can support a claim about a concrete task?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

A relevant sample or observation with the conditions and contribution documented.

</details>

### Rubrics

Use independent criteria with observable performance descriptors. Calibrate assessors against common examples and attach the rubric version to each record.

**Example** — Anchor evidence quality by stating what counts as a relevant source and when uncertainty should be noted.

**Limitation** — Highly detailed rubrics can burden reviewers and obscure overall judgment.

**Knowledge check** — Why version rubric criteria?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

So later reviewers can interpret earlier decisions under the criteria used at the time.

</details>

### Reliability

Compare how consistently assessors interpret the same evidence and repeat the process with diverse samples. Investigate disagreements before using scores for consequential decisions.

**Example** — Two assessors independently score the same diagnostic transcript and discuss differences.

**Limitation** — Agreement can coexist with shared bias or a poor measurement target.

**Knowledge check** — What does high reviewer agreement establish?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Consistency for the assessed samples, with validity requiring independent evidence.

</details>

### Bias

Look for irrelevant barriers, uneven access and criteria that reward presentation style unrelated to the work. Provide accommodations with consistent evidence standards.

**Example** — Offer equivalent oral and written formats when the skill is diagnosing faults rather than writing.

**Limitation** — Alternate formats still require checking that they permit comparable evidence.

**Knowledge check** — When is polished writing an appropriate criterion?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

When written communication is genuinely part of the work being assessed.

</details>

### Appeals

Preserve the original rubric and evidence, tell learners how to challenge a decision and assign review to someone equipped to reconsider it. Record the outcome and rationale.

**Example** — A learner submits a missing diagnostic log and the original record remains intact alongside the review.

**Limitation** — An appeal process needs capacity and a realistic response time.

**Knowledge check** — What should a reviewer preserve?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

The original assessment, rubric version, evidence and the decision trail.

</details>

## Challenges

### Compare evidence sources

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

A self-rating is high, a test score modest and one work sample strong.

**Work product** — An evidence comparison with confidence and next evidence requested.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare what each source measures and under what conditions.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Triangulate and state the limits of each source. Reconsider your approach. Compare what each source measures and under what conditions.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>

### Construct a rubric

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

A reviewer needs consistent criteria for troubleshooting decisions.

**Work product** — Three anchored criteria and a calibration example.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each level should describe observable behavior.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Anchor criteria with observable evidence. Reconsider your approach. Each level should describe observable behavior.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>

### Review contradictory evidence

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

A learner disputes a score after finding an omitted work sample.

**Work product** — An appeal record, review steps and bounded reassessment plan.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Keep the original version available for review.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Preserve records and allow independent review. Reconsider your approach. Keep the original version available for review.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>
