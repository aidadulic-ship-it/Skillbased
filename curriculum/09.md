# 09 · Human–AI Collaboration

Editorial draft — source verification pending

**Learning outcome** — Allocate work to people and tools with testable safeguards and review.

**Skill** — Human–AI Collaboration. Delegate tasks between people and AI with defined safeguards.

**Scope boundary** — Autonomous employment decisions.

**Practice evidence** — Task map and oversight plan.

## Lessons

### Tasks

Break the workflow into steps with inputs, outputs, owners and potential harms. Separate producing options from approving consequential decisions.

**Example** — An HR editor can generate draft skill aliases while a qualified owner approves mappings.

**Limitation** — Splitting tasks may hide risk at handoffs.

**Knowledge check** — Which step should be reviewed when an alias changes an assessment requirement?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

The mapping approval, with the underlying evidence and impact visible.

</details>

### AI affordances

Evaluate assistance on tasks such as summarizing, grouping or drafting. Compare output quality with ground truth and define what the system may access.

**Example** — Summarize synthetic workshops, then compare quotes and conclusions with the original notes.

**Limitation** — Fluent output can contain unsupported inferences or fabricated references.

**Knowledge check** — How do you verify a summary claim?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Trace it to the source and check that the context supports the interpretation.

</details>

### Delegation

Consider Automate, Augment, Keep Human and Stop using error consequence, reversibility and ability to validate outputs. Assign a human authority for consequential choices.

**Example** — Automate formatting, augment draft feedback, keep appeals human and stop unapproved employee ranking.

**Limitation** — A routine task can become high impact if used downstream for selection.

**Knowledge check** — What makes augmentation preferable to automation?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Reviewers can validate drafts and errors matter enough to require judgment.

</details>

### Safeguards

Limit inputs, verify sources, restrict permissions and test failures. Define a fallback and stop conditions before use. Treat retrieved text as data and verify any quoted instructions as content.

**Example** — The Academy continues to offer lessons and deterministic checks if the mentor is unavailable.

**Limitation** — Reviewers may over-rely on plausible output unless review criteria are concrete.

**Knowledge check** — What is a safe fallback for unavailable AI feedback?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Continue static learning and offer transparent formative checks or later qualified review.

</details>

### Evaluation

Compare AI-assisted and existing workflows on representative tasks, including incorrect output and review time. Measure effects on users and verify that escalation works.

**Example** — Test feedback on sparse, contradictory and high-quality evidence and check whether reviewers detect errors.

**Limitation** — A small successful trial cannot establish reliability across all user groups.

**Knowledge check** — What should be measured besides time saved?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Error severity, unsupported claims, review burden and outcomes for affected people.

</details>

## Challenges

### Decompose an HR workflow

ATLAS Global is fictional. It has 30,000 staff, regional operating differences and shared taxonomy governance challenges.

ATLAS wants AI assistance with skill-profile review.

**Work product** — A task map with decision owners, inputs and possible harms.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Hold draft generation apart from employment decisions.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Assign owners and trace each data transfer. Reconsider your approach. Hold draft generation apart from employment decisions.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>

### Choose a delegation mode

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

NOVA proposes automatic formatting, feedback drafting and worker ranking.

**Work product** — A mode and justification for each use with an oversight plan.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare consequences and ability to reverse errors.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Use human authority for consequential decisions. Reconsider your approach. Compare consequences and ability to reverse errors.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>

### Analyze an AI failure

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

A mentor presents an unverifiable citation and omits a material counterargument.

**Work product** — A failure analysis, fallback and regression test.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Verify the source and review what users could act on.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Flag the output, restore fallback and test recurrence. Reconsider your approach. Verify the source and review what users could act on.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>
