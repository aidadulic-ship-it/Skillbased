# 10 · Skills Governance & Ethics

Editorial draft — source verification pending

**Learning outcome** — Set enforceable limits on skills data, challenge unfair outcomes and assign remedies.

**Skill** — Skills Governance & Ethics. Define permissible use, accountability and remedies for skills information.

**Scope boundary** — Legal determinations.

**Practice evidence** — Governance and incident plan.

## Lessons

### Purpose limitation

Describe the use case, necessary fields, retention period and permitted users before collecting data. Review any new use with its own justification.

**Example** — NOVA limits practice logs to development planning during the pilot and records who can access them.

**Limitation** — Reuse for employment ranking raises new fairness and legal questions.

**Knowledge check** — What triggers a purpose review?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

A proposal to reuse data for a materially different decision.

</details>

### Access

Use role-based access for each purpose, separate administrative duties and review access changes. Audit aggregate patterns without collecting unnecessary personal content.

**Example** — A mentor sees assigned practice evidence while a reporting role receives aggregate counts.

**Limitation** — Small aggregate groups can reveal individual identities.

**Knowledge check** — Who should see an individual practice result?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

People whose role requires it for the stated purpose, subject to approved access rules.

</details>

### Fairness

Inspect how evidence opportunities, criteria and decision outcomes vary across contexts. Involve affected people and test corrections with comparable examples.

**Example** — ATLAS checks whether equivalent evidence is accepted consistently across sites.

**Limitation** — A statistically similar selection rate does not resolve all individual fairness concerns.

**Knowledge check** — What issue could a lower evidence rate indicate?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Unequal access to work or observation opportunities, among other plausible causes.

</details>

### Accountability

Document responsibility for approvals, data corrections, incident response and oversight. Make it possible to identify the policy and decision in force at a given time.

**Example** — An editor drafts a change while a named owner signs off before publication.

**Limitation** — A named owner still needs authority and time to act.

**Knowledge check** — What should an approval record contain?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

The decision, approver, time, version and scope.

</details>

### Audit and remedy

Investigate with preserved evidence, proportionate access and a clear process to correct decisions. Track implementation of remedies and verify whether the issue recurs.

**Example** — Pause an affected use, preserve rubric versions and reassess decisions after correcting access bias.

**Limitation** — Suspending a process may require interim arrangements for affected employees.

**Knowledge check** — What should happen after a remedy is implemented?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answer</summary>

Verify its effect, check for recurrence and record the decision trail.

</details>

## Challenges

### Assess profile risks

ATLAS Global is fictional. It has 30,000 staff, regional operating differences and shared taxonomy governance challenges.

ATLAS wants company-wide profiles assembled from many regional systems.

**Work product** — A risk register with data minimization and worker correction measures.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Ask how each collected field supports a specific decision.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Limit collection and document access and retention. Reconsider your approach. Ask how each collected field supports a specific decision.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>

### Draft governance

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

NOVA wants to scale beyond the pilot after leadership sign-off.

**Work product** — A governance blueprint covering approvals, review, correction and deletion.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Assign decision authority and a review trigger.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Define owners, decisions and remedies. Reconsider your approach. Assign decision authority and a review trigger.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>

### Investigate unfair assessment

NOVA Manufacturing is fictional. It has 180 staff at two sites and is considering a six-month skills pilot during modernization. HR and manager capacity are limited.

One worker group repeatedly fails a rubric criterion tied to prior assignment access.

**Work product** — An investigation plan, interim safeguard and proposed remedy.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Preserve records and inspect how chances to demonstrate the skill differ.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Review criteria · Rubric 1.0</summary>

- **Decision quality** — Review access and criteria before resuming consequential use. Reconsider your approach. Preserve records and inspect how chances to demonstrate the skill differ.
- **Evidence quality** — Use scenario evidence and flag important missing information. Identify a specific observation and explain what remains uncertain.
- **Tradeoffs and next step** — Name a tradeoff, an owner and a review condition. State who acts, what might go wrong and when the decision should be revisited.

</details>
